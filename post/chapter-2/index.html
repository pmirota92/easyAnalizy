#002: Are All Horoscopes Positive? (NLP + Sentiment + Similarity Analysis)!

November 7, 2024

Project Overview

This project investigates whether daily horoscopes are genuinely positive, whether different zodiac signs receive varying emotional tones, and how often horoscope texts are reused. I collected 3 years of daily horoscopes across all 12 zodiac signs and applied modern NLP techniques to analyze sentiment, repetition, and thematic structure.

Core techniques:

  • Web scraping (3-year dataset, 12 signs)
  • Sentiment analysis (VADER + Hugging Face transformer)
  • Semantic similarity (MiniLM embeddings + cosine similarity)
  • Statistical testing (chi-square, post-hoc, bootstrapping)
  • NLP EDA (word clouds, timelines, bar plots)

1. Motivation & Research Questions

I explored a long-standing question:

Are horoscopes always positive or neutral? Or do some signs receive more negativity?

Key hypotheses examined:

  1. Most horoscopes are positive/neutral (H0), with few negative cases.
  2. Some zodiac signs may receive more negative content than others.
  3. Horoscope text may be reused or recycled across days.
  4. Capricorn and Leo sentiment trends may differ from the overall baseline.

2. Data Collection & Scope

  • Source: Public horoscope website

  • Period: 3 years × 12 signs

  • Granularity: One text per sign per day

  • Processing:

    • normalization (lowercasing, boilerplate removal)
    • tokenization & lemmatization
    • handling missing entries

  • Metadata: dates, zodiac sign, sentiment labels, embeddings, similarity metrics

This produced a clean, structured dataset suitable for NLP analysis and time-series sentiment tracking.

3. NLP Pipeline & Sentiment Analysis

Sentiment Methods Used

  • VADER → rule-based, robust for short social-style text
  • Hugging Face Transformer Model → refined classification into positive, neutral, negative

This dual approach allowed reliable sentiment estimation even for short horoscope texts.

Visual Outputs

Findings revealed that although positivity dominates, two signs consistently show higher negative sentiment rates, contradicting the belief that “all horoscopes are uplifting.”

5. Statistical Testing

Chi-square tests

  • Assessed if negative sentiment rates differ across signs
  • Result: significant differences (p < 0.05)

Post-hoc testing

  • Identified two signs responsible for most negativity

Bootstrapping

  • Estimated confidence intervals for per-sign negativity rates

Example:

Removing the two outlier signs removes statistical significance, proving negativity is not evenly distributed.

6. Text Similarity Analysis (Semantic Reuse Detection)

Method

  • Sentence embeddings using paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2
  • Cosine similarity to compare each horoscope with earlier ones for the same sign

Findings:

  • More than 70% of new texts closely resemble at least one earlier message
  • Strong evidence of recycled or templated writing

7. Key Results

  • Most horoscopes are positive or neutral
  • Two zodiac signs receive significantly more negative content
  • Horoscopes are often repetitive — many texts closely match earlier ones
  • Emotional tone is not uniformly distributed across signs

8. Limitations

  • Sentiment models can misinterpret short metaphorical text
  • Analysis is based on one source, not the entire astrology ecosystem
  • Short text length increases sensitivity to small lexical changes
  • Results are descriptive, not predictive or causal

9. Future Work

  • Scrape multiple sites for cross-source comparison
  • Use domain-adapted sentiment models (finetuned transformers)
  • Add topic modeling (LDA, BERTopic) to detect recurring themes
  • Build an interactive dashboard (Streamlit) to explore sign-level trends
